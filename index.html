    Assistant or agent? What Anthropic's merger of chat and Cowork moved across the line, and what it left in the user's hands

    If you have ever let an AI tool act for you, or are deciding whether to, this is where the line between answering and acting now runs, who draws it, and the four settings that decide what happens in your name.

    On 16 September 2026 Anthropic stopped asking users to choose between chat and Cowork: one Claude now decides whether a request needs an answer or an errand. The line between assistant and agent did not vanish; it moved into four settings (what Claude can reach, how often it must ask, what it can never undo silently, in whose name it acts) and a classifier that reviews actions. Anthropic's own numbers explain why: users approve 97 % of permission prompts and catch 13.6 % of dangerous commands where the classifier catches 89 %, while browser prompt-injection success fell from 23.6 % to below 0.1 % in a year. Consent moves from the act to the boundary, responsibility stays with the human, and the meter becomes the last brake.
